The appraisal process

What does an appraiser actually do?

An appraisal is an analysis of market evidence used to develop an opinion of value for a defined purpose and effective date.

What happens during the inspection?

Inspection procedures vary by assignment. The appraiser observes relevant property characteristics and documents information needed for analysis and reporting.

How are comparable sales selected?

Comparable selection considers factors such as location, property characteristics, market area, sale date, physical similarity, and the quality and relevance of available market evidence.

Understanding value

Market value vs. assessed value

Market value and a property's tax assessment are related to real estate but are not interchangeable concepts.

Appraisal vs. CMA vs. BPO

These are different valuation products prepared for different purposes and under different standards or scopes.

Why can two appraisals differ?

Appraisal is an analytical process involving judgment and reconciliation. Different assignment conditions, data availability, comparable selection, and market interpretation can produce different credible opinions.

Do renovations automatically add their cost to value?

No. Cost and contributory value are not necessarily the same. The market's reaction to an improvement matters.

Why can an appraisal differ from an asking price or Zestimate?

Asking prices, automated estimates, and appraisals are different products based on different data and methodologies.

Estate, trust, divorce & retrospective valuation

Date-of-death / retrospective value

A retrospective appraisal develops an opinion of value as of a specified historical effective date rather than the current date.

Estate & trust assignments
